# Baileigh Metal Horizontal Bandsaw BS-210H Standard Operating Procedure

📄 [Download Official Manufacturer Manual (DOCX)](manual.docx)

---


## SAFETY FIRST (READ BEFORE USE)

Coolant System: Ensure the coolant pump is ON and flowing to prevent the blade from overheating and snapping.  
Vise Security: Always clamp the metal workpiece tightly in the vise; never hold metal by hand.  
Blade Guard: Keep the adjustable blade guide as close to the workpiece as possible for maximum support.  

## BASIC OPERATION

Open the vise, insert material, and tighten the handwheel.  
Set the descent rate (hydraulic feed) so the saw drops slowly into the metal.  
Turn the power ON.  
The saw will automatically shut off once the cut is complete.  

## TROUBLESHOOTING

Machine Will Not Start  
Check E-Stop: Ensure the mushroom-style Emergency Stop button is twisted and released.  
Trigger Switch: For manual operation, the trigger switch on the handle must be fully depressed. For hydraulic descent, ensure the selector switch is set to the correct mode.  
Limit Switch: The saw features an automatic shut-off switch that triggers when the arm reaches the bottom of its travel. If the arm is already down, the motor will not start.  
Blade Tension Switch: Many Baileigh models have a safety sensor that prevents the motor from running if the blade is too loose. Check the tension gauge.  
Blade Is Stalling or Jumping  
Check Tension: If the blade "slips" on the drive wheels under load, increase the tension using the handwheel until the gauge reaches the green zone.  
Speed Selection: You may be using a speed that is too high for the material. Shift to a lower gear/speed setting for harder metals like stainless steel.  
Drive Belt: Open the motor access panel (unplugged) to ensure the V-belt is tight and not glazed or slipping.  
Inaccurate or Crooked Cuts  
Blade Guides: Ensure the adjustable sliding blade guide is moved as close to the workpiece as possible. This minimizes blade twist and deflection.  
Guide Bearings: Inspect the ceramic or roller guides. They should be snug against the blade without binding it. If there is a gap, the blade will wander.  
Feed Rate: If the hydraulic descent is set too fast, the blade will "lead" or curve. Turn the hydraulic flow control valve clockwise to slow the descent.  
Excessive Heat or Blade Wear  
Coolant System: Verify the coolant pump is running and the nozzle is aimed at the entry point of the cut. Check the reservoir for metal "chips" blocking the pump intake.  
TPI (Teeth Per Inch): Ensure you are following the "3-Tooth Rule"—at least three teeth must be in contact with the material at all times. If the teeth are too large for thin-walled tubing, they will strip off.  
When in Doubt  
STOP the machine: If the blade snaps or the material slips out of the vise, hit the E-Stop immediately.  
Secure the Vise: Never attempt to "hold" metal by hand. If the piece moves, it will snap the blade and potentially cause injury.  
Hydraulic Lock: Always lock the hydraulic cylinder before changing blades to prevent the heavy saw arm from falling unexpectedly.  
Ask for help: Metal bandsaws use high-tension blades and heavy castings; if the descent is erratic, consult a supervisor for hydraulic adjustment.
