## SAFETY FIRST (READ BEFORE USE)

Eye Protection: Always wear safety glasses or a face shield when operating the saw.  
Fingers Clear: Keep your fingers to the side of the blade path, never directly in front of it.  
Tension Check: Ensure the blade is properly tensioned before turning the machine on; a loose blade can snap or wander.  
Hold-Down Foot: Adjust the hold-down foot so it rests lightly on top of your workpiece to prevent the wood from lifting.  

## BASIC OPERATION


### Power-On Sequence:

Check that the blade teeth are pointing downward.  
Adjust the air blower nozzle to clear sawdust from your cut line.  
Turn the speed dial to the lowest setting before flipping the power switch ON.  
Gradually increase speed based on the thickness and hardness of the material.  

### Blade Changes:

Turn OFF and unplug the saw.  
Release the tension lever.  
Loosen the thumb screws on the upper and lower blade holders to swap the blade.  

## TROUBLESHOOTING

Machine Will Not Start  
Check Power Switch: Ensure the power switch is in the ON position and the unit is plugged into a functional 120V outlet.  
Variable Speed Dial: If the dial is set to the absolute minimum, some motors may stall. Turn the dial to a medium setting and try again.  
Safety Key: Many WEN models have a removable yellow plastic safety key in the power switch. If it is missing, the saw will not engage.  
Blade Keeps Breaking  
Over-Tensioning: If the blade snaps immediately upon starting, the tension lever is likely set too tight. It should "ping" like a guitar string, not feel brittle.  
Aggressive Feeding: Do not force the wood into the blade. If you have to push hard, the blade is either dull or you are moving too fast for the material thickness.  
Blade Orientation: Ensure the teeth are pointing DOWN toward the table. If they point up, the workpiece will chatter violently and snap the blade.  
Excessive Vibration or Workpiece "Jumping"  
Hold-Down Foot: Adjust the metal hold-down foot so it rests lightly on top of your wood. If there is a gap, the wood will chatter against the table.  
Table Bolt: Check the locking knob under the table. If the table is not locked at 0° (or your desired angle), it will vibrate and create inaccurate cuts.  
Mounting: Ensure the saw is bolted to a sturdy workbench or stand. Benchtop scroll saws are prone to "walking" if not secured.  
Air Blower Not Clearing Dust  
Tube Alignment: Ensure the flexible plastic air tube is pointed directly at the contact point between the blade and the wood.  
Kinked Hose: Check the internal bellows and the clear tubing for kinks or disconnects that prevent air from reaching the nozzle.  
Blade Slipping Out of Clamps  
Clean the Clamps: Sawdust or oil on the blade ends can cause them to slip. Wipe the blade ends and the inside of the clamps with a dry cloth.  
Tighten Thumb Screws: If using pinless blades, ensure the setscrews are tightened firmly with the provided hex key or thumb knob.  
When in Doubt  
STOP the machine: If the blade breaks, turn off the power before reaching in to retrieve the broken pieces.  
Unplug for Maintenance: Always unplug the saw before changing blades or adjusting internal components.  
Check Blade Type: Ensure you are using the correct blade (Pinned vs. Pinless) for your specific holder setup.  
Ask for help: If the motor hums but the arm doesn't move, the internal linkage may be jammed; consult a supervisor.
