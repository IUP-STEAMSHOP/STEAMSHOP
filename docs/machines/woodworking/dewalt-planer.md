## SAFETY FIRST (READ BEFORE USE)

Ear Protection: This machine is extremely loud; hearing protection is mandatory.  
Nails/Hardware: Inspect all wood for nails, staples, or dirt before feeding; hitting metal will ruin the blades instantly.  
No Reaching In: Never reach into the planer while it is running.  
Material Length: Do not plane boards shorter than 12 inches to avoid them getting stuck or kicked back.  

## BASIC OPERATION

Measure the thickest part of your board.  
Set the planer height to roughly 1/32" less than the board thickness.  
Turn on the dust extractor (required to prevent chip buildup).  
Start the motor and feed the board into the front of the machine; let the rollers pull it through.  

## TROUBLESHOOTING

Machine Will Not Start  
Check the Circuit Breaker: The planer has an onboard circuit breaker (usually a small button near the power switch). If the machine was overloaded, press this button to reset it.  
Power Switch Lock: Ensure the yellow safety key is fully inserted into the paddle switch.  
Overheating: If the motor shuts off during heavy use, allow it to cool for 15–20 minutes before attempting to restart.  
Boards Are Not Feeding (Getting Stuck)  
Clean the Tables: Sap and dust buildup on the infeed and outfeed tables create friction. Clean the tables with a degreaser and apply a thin coat of paste wax.  
Clean the Rollers: Unplug the machine and wipe the rubber feed rollers with a cloth dampened with denatured alcohol to restore their "grip."  
Depth of Cut: You may be trying to remove too much material. Reduce the depth of cut (aim for 1/32" or less for wide or hard boards).  
Table Alignment: Ensure the extension tables are level with the main granite/steel bed. If they sag, the board will "nose dive" and stop feeding.  
Poor Surface Quality (Snipe or Ridges)  
Snipe (Gouge at ends): This is caused by the board tilting as it enters or leaves the machine. Lift the far end of the board slightly as it enters, or use "sacrificial" scrap wood before and after your workpiece.  
Ridges/Lines: This indicates a nick in the knives (usually from hitting a staple or sand). The DeWALT 735 has two-sided knives; you can shift one knife slightly to the left or right to offset the nicks, or flip/replace them.  
Tear-out: If the wood grain is fuzzy or chipped, try feeding the board in the opposite direction or reduce the feed speed (if using the 2-speed Model 735).  
Excessive Dust or Chips Blowing Out the Front  
Dust Collection: This machine has an internal fan that forces chips out. If you are not connected to a vacuum or dust collector, the chips will clog the cutter head and blow back at you.  
Clogged Chute: Unplug the machine and remove the dust shroud to check for "chip packing," which happens if the vacuum is too weak or the wood is very wet.  
When in Doubt  
STOP the machine: If you hear a high-pitched scream or the motor "bogs down" significantly, hit the stop paddle immediately.  
Unplug for Maintenance: Never reach near the cutter head or change knives without physically disconnecting the power cord.  
Check Material: Never plane wood that has paint, glue, or embedded metal.  
Ask for help: Changing the three knives on a DeWALT planer requires precision; if the cuts are uneven, consult a supervisor for blade replacement
