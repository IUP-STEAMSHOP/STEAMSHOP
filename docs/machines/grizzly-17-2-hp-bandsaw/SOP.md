# Grizzly 17" 2 HP Bandsaw Standard Operating Procedure

## SAFETY FIRST (READ BEFORE USE)

Blade Guard Height: Adjust the upper blade guide to be no more than 1/4" above the material.  
Entanglement: Tie back long hair and avoid loose clothing; do not wear gloves.  
Push Sticks: Use a push stick for any cuts where your fingers would come within 3 inches of the blade.  
Wait for Stop: Never back a piece of wood out of a cut while the blade is moving, as it can pull the blade off the wheels.  

## BASIC OPERATION

Open the wheel covers and check blade tracking/tension (ensure the saw is unplugged).  
Turn on the dust collection system.  
Flip the switch to ON and allow the blade to reach full speed.  
Feed the material slowly, allowing the blade to cut without forcing it.  

## TROUBLESHOOTING

Machine Has Power but Won't Move  
Check E-Stop: Verify that the red Emergency Stop button is not depressed.  
Door Safety Switches: Ensure both the upper and lower wheel covers are completely closed and latched. This saw features interlock switches that prevent the motor from starting if the doors are ajar.  
Foot Brake: Ensure the foot brake is fully released. If the brake is engaged, the internal safety switch will disconnect power to the motor.  
Power Reset: Turn the main power switch to OFF, wait 30 seconds, and turn it back ON to reset the magnetic starter.  
Blade Wandering or Inaccurate Cuts  
Check Tension: Verify the blade is tensioned correctly for its width using the scale on the upper wheel. A loose blade will "bow" and wander during thick cuts.  
Guide Bearings: Ensure the upper and lower guide bearings are adjusted to roughly 0.004" (the thickness of a piece of paper) from the blade.  
Blade Guard Height: Confirm the guard is set no more than 1/4" above the material. Excessively high guards allow the blade to twist.  
Dull Blade: If the blade requires excessive force to cut or pulls to one side despite proper setup, the teeth are likely dull. Replace the blade.  
Excessive Vibration or Noise  
Blade Tracking: Turn the machine OFF and rotate the wheels by hand to ensure the blade is centered on the tires.  
Wheel Tires: Check for built-up sawdust or "pitch" on the rubber tires. Clean them with a stiff brush; debris causes the blade to "bounce."  
Drive Belt: Inspect the V-belt for cracks, slipping, or debris.  
Motor Bogs Down or Smokes  
Feed Rate: Slow down. Let the speed of the blade do the work without forcing the wood.  
Dust Collection: Ensure the dust collector is ON. Packed sawdust in the lower cabinet creates friction against the lower wheel and can cause the motor to overheat.  
Incorrect TPI: Ensure you are using the correct Teeth Per Inch (TPI) for your material. Fewer teeth (3-4 TPI) are required for thick resawing to prevent heat buildup.  
When in Doubt  
STOP the machine: If you hear a loud "bang" (likely a snapped blade), immediately step back and hit the E-Stop or foot brake.  
Power down safely: Unplug the machine before opening wheel covers or performing any blade changes.  
Ask a supervisor: Bandsaws require precise "tuning." If you are unsure about tracking or tension, ask for a trained operator's assistance.  
Never "test" fixes: Do not attempt to adjust guide bearings while the blade is in motion.
