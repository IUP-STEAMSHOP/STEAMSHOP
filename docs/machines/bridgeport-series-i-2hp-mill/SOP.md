## SAFETY FIRST (READ BEFORE USE)

Spindle Brake: Ensure the spindle is at a complete stop before changing tools or touching the workpiece.  
Entanglement: Absolutely no gloves, loose sleeves, or jewelry when operating the manual mill.  
Clearance: Ensure the quill is locked or fully retracted when not in use.  
Clamping: Ensure the vise or workpiece is securely bolted to the table; use a T-handle wrench to tighten.  

## BASIC OPERATION


### Changing Speeds:

Note: Only change the variable speed dial while the motor is RUNNING.  
Shift between High and Low range only when the motor is STOPPED.  

### Power Feed (If Equipped):

Check travel limits to ensure the table will not hit the end-stops.  
Engage the directional lever and adjust the feed rate dial slowly.  
Troubleshooting  
Spindle Will Not Start  
Check Power: Ensure the main disconnect switch is in the ON position.  
Emergency Stop: Verify the E-Stop button (if equipped) is released.  
Forward/Reverse Switch: Ensure the drum switch is fully engaged in either Forward or Reverse; if left in the middle, the motor will not receive power.  
Thermal Overload: If the motor is hot and won't start, the internal thermal overload may have tripped; allow it to cool and reset.  
Machine Vibrating or Making Loud Noise  
Speed Range: Confirm the Hi-Low range lever is fully seated; a partially engaged gear will cause grinding noises.  
Drive Belt: Inspect the variable speed drive belt for fraying or flat spots.  
Tooling: Ensure the R8 collet is tightened properly with the drawbar; loose tooling causes severe chatter.  
Quill Lock: Ensure the quill lock is secure if you are not performing a drilling operation.  
Poor Surface Finish (Chatter Marks)  
Check Sharpness: Replace dull end mills or inserts; dull tools increase cutting pressure and vibration.  
Spindle Speed: Adjust the variable speed dial—often, reducing the RPM will eliminate harmonic chatter.  
Workholding: Ensure the vise is bolted securely to the table and the workpiece is clamped tightly.  
Gib Adjustment: If the table has excessive "play," the gibs may need to be tightened by a supervisor.  
Quill is Hard to Move or Sticking  
Lubrication: Apply a small amount of machine oil to the quill surface and move it up and down to distribute.  
Clock Spring: If the quill does not return to the top automatically, the clock spring may be broken or disconnected.  
Lock Engagement: Ensure the quill lock handle is completely released.  
When in Doubt  
STOP the machine: Engage the spindle brake immediately if you hear a crash or unusual grinding.  
Power down safely: Turn off the motor before attempting to clear chips or adjust tooling.  
Ask a supervisor: Manual mills require precise tactile feedback; if it feels "wrong," ask for a trained operator’s assistance.  
Never "test" fixes: Do not attempt to adjust the variable speed dial unless the motor is already running.
