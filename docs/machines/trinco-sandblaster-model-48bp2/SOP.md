## SAFETY FIRST (READ BEFORE USE)

Seal Check: Ensure the door latches are secure before activating the air blast to prevent dust from entering the room.  
Window Guard: Do not operate if the viewing window is too frosted to see the workpiece clearly.  
Gloves: Use only the integrated heavy-duty rubber gloves provided with the cabinet.  

## BASIC OPERATION

Place the part inside the cabinet and close the door securely.  
Turn on the internal light and the dust collector/vacuum.  
Insert your arms into the gloves and grasp the blast nozzle and the part.  
Depress the foot pedal to start the flow of abrasive; move the nozzle in a steady motion across the part.  

## TROUBLESHOOTING

Low Blasting Pressure / Weak Stream  
Check Air Supply: Ensure the shop compressor is on and the regulator is set to the correct PSI (typically 60-100 PSI).  
Clogged Nozzle: Unplug the air and inspect the ceramic nozzle. Debris or oversized media can block the tip.  
Foot Pedal: Ensure the foot pedal is fully depressed and the air lines leading to it are not kinked or leaking.  
Moisture in Air: If the stream is "pulsing" or "spitting," there is water in the lines. Drain the water trap/filter located on the back of the machine.  
Media Not Flowing (Air Only, No Abrasive)  
Check Media Level: Ensure there is enough abrasive in the hopper. The suction tube must be submerged in the media.  
Damp Media: If the abrasive has absorbed moisture, it will "bridge" in the hopper and won't flow. Replace with dry media or sift the existing batch.  
Suction Tube Blockage: Inspect the pickup tube at the bottom of the hopper for clogs or large debris (like paint chips or rust flakes).  
Mixing Valve: Adjust the air/media mixing valve at the base of the hopper to ensure the ratio is correct.  
Poor Visibility Inside Cabinet  
Dust Collector: Ensure the BP2 dust collector (vacuum) is ON. If it is running but the cabinet is still cloudy, the filter bag or cartridge is likely full and needs to be shaken out or replaced.  
Worn Window Shield: The plastic "under-sheet" of the viewing window becomes frosted over time. If you cannot see the workpiece, replace the sacrificial plastic film.  
Internal Light: Check the fluorescent or LED bulb inside the cabinet. If it’s out, the media may have damaged the protective cover.  
Media Leaking from Cabinet  
Door Seals: Inspect the rubber gaskets around the doors. If they are torn or brittle, dust will escape.  
Glove Holes: Check the integrated gloves for tears. Even a small hole will spray high-pressure dust onto the operator.  
Negative Pressure: Ensure the dust collector is pulling enough air to maintain negative pressure inside the cabinet, which keeps dust from leaking out of small gaps.  
When in Doubt  
STOP the machine: Release the foot pedal immediately if a glove fails or the window breaks.  
Static Shock: Sandblasting creates significant static electricity. Ensure the machine is properly grounded to avoid shocks.  
Never Open While Blasting: Wait for the dust to settle and the vacuum to clear the air before opening the side doors.  
Ask for help: If the blast stream is inconsistent despite a full hopper, the internal abrasive hose may be worn through and require replacement by a supervisor.
